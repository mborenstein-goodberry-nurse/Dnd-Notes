## Log

### The engine answers a strange request

After the company returned to [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Foo Fighters|Foo Fighters]] territory, an experiment with the recovered life-engine took a decidedly unexpected turn. **Zach's April 25 notes record that the two hemispheres were put together**, a change from the separate pieces held at the end of [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/Session Journal/Leaving the Battlefield|the previous session]]. The halves were now joined as one artifact. **Wiley later said he believes Jonks assembled them**, although this attribution remains tentative; he recalled that the joined pieces then **floated around**, without a dramatic immediate effect. The surviving recording begins with someone concentrating on an orb-like artifact and asking it to reshape reality by producing a sandwich. An exceptional Arcana result seems to make it work: dirt briefly takes the shape of a sandwich, only for the food to shiver and collapse back into soil as the experimenter reaches for it.

A voice associated with the object appears to reject this petty use of its power, implying that it has shaped the world and should be called upon for something more consequential. The assembled object and its capabilities provoke unease rather than a clear breakthrough. **The identity of the speaker and the full consequences of joining the hemispheres remain uncertain.** The group also reflects on the previous night's conflict: [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Diane|Diane]] had been badly triggered by the shared body's dragon-like fire, and the relationship between the companions remains strained.

Both **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Arthur Morgan|Arthur]]** and **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Thornwick|Thornwick]]** step aside for what the table calls much-needed mental-health days. The remaining company continues toward the [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Infinite Mausoleum|Infinite Mausoleum]], which the travelers believe may hold objects or knowledge connected to their aims. One companion invokes the ancestors as a reason to seek the site. The cart is back in the party's possession, allowing the journey to proceed more easily.

### A goblinoid settlement of the Meshites

Travel northeast from [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Foo Fighters|Foo Fighters]] territory brings the group to a lively goblinoid settlement. Old signs for **Globby Glore** have been crossed out and replaced with the name heard as **Machaimia** <span class="new-tag">new</span> (the DM spells letters resembling M-A-C-H-A-I-M-I-A); residents call themselves the **Meshites**. **Later retrospective clarification:** Wiley *believes* Machaimia refers to a **religion Noisy Owlbear started**, not conclusively to the settlement's proper name. The audio and signage remain evidence of the words encountered, but not of an official city renaming. The streets are busy with orcs, goblins and hobgoblins, welcoming visitors rather than threatening them.

At a magic-item shop, the company encounters **[[Worlds/Astodan/People/Zoff|Zoff]]**, the legless goblin merchant previously encountered by [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Grayson|Grayson]]. He advertises enchantments and shows off a supposed [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Treasure/Deck of Many Things|Deck of Many Things]], though this particular deck's authenticity and pricing are far from clear. The party does **not** acquire it. Zoff had previously sold Grayson a [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/Other/Counterfeit Deck of Many Things|counterfeit deck]]; that earlier deception does not conclusively establish the nature of this new display. He recommends an enchanter associated with his extended kin, whose roaming caravan operates near the [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Infinite Mausoleum|Infinite Mausoleum]]; the merchant himself claims to have lost his legs during an expedition inside the mausoleum. The companions leave with reason to be wary of his bargains, but also with a possible contact ahead.

In search of a way to see in darkness, the party is offered something advertised as [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Treasure/Goggles of Night|Goggles of Night]]. On inspection, the eyewear seems to be little more than magical or magically presented tinted sunglasses, **not reliably the standard item**. Another companion uses [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Prestidigitation|Prestidigitation]] to darken the shop and test the claim. The group repeatedly employs [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Identify|Identify]] to examine the merchant's wares, undermining some of his salesmanship. Among the items under consideration are an unusual **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/Other/Ring of Spelling|Ring of Spelling]]** (distinct from a Ring of Spell Storing) and a **glass cube of force**. A deal is struck involving the ring and cube, apparently with the group splitting the cost; someone also buys the dubious goggles. The ring's letter-changing function becomes clear later, but the other purchases' precise properties and all final owners are **not securely established by this recording**. They should not automatically be equated with similarly named standard items.

### Mirage, a stranger made of metal

An unusually small shape brushes past [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Isabelle|Isabelle]]'s leg in the shop. It resembles a tiny, one-eyed metallic creature and at first tries to slip away rather than attract notice. Some companions follow it into the street. Once outside, it changes from tiny to merely small, and the party learns that it is **not an ordinary mindless machine**: it has a soul bound within it. The nearby mechanical dog is brought into the interaction, but communication remains awkward.

The creature introduces itself as **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Mirage|Mirage]]** <span class="new-tag">new</span>, says that it seeks magical treasure, and offers to accompany the travelers to the [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Infinite Mausoleum|Infinite Mausoleum]]. The company accepts. Its origin, the meaning of its soul-binding, and the limits of its shape-changing are not explained during this encounter. In the recording, [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Mirage|Mirage]] is clearly a new party participant, though the nature of its soul-binding remains unresolved.

### Jingles and the tavern incident

The company stops at a tavern near the mausoleum to rest and consider its limited funds. There it encounters **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Jingles|Jingles]]**, an autumn eladrin clown entertaining children while apparently being kept in compulsory service. Speaking privately, Jingles says he owes substantial debts and asks for help escaping the owner who watches him. The account of his debt and earlier misadventures is exaggerated and often played for laughs, but his inability to leave appears genuine.

A companion stages a distraction by striking the tavern owner with a ram-like headbutt, knocking him unconscious. The distractor receives [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Guidance|Guidance]] to bolster the attempt. Another searches the owner's pockets and takes a small silver key. The attempt to pass off the unconscious owner as merely drunk goes badly, causing a commotion among the children and other patrons. Outside, [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Jingles|Jingles]] uses the key to remove his restraints and agrees to travel with the group. The new arrival is an unpredictable jokester, but is no longer being held by the tavern owner.

The group rests, attends to its newly acquired items, and leaves in the morning. The DM describes some concern among an ancestral presence about both the orb and the decision to free the clown. [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Weary|Weary]] and [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Diane|Diane]] also make Religion checks connected to their own spiritual ties, but the full meaning of their results is not disclosed here.

### A market before the mausoleum

By the following day's travel, the group reaches the **[[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Infinite Mausoleum|Infinite Mausoleum]]**, an imposing structure with huge iron-and-wood doors and a small collection of tents resembling a market outside. Traders and enchanters advertise wares to would-be entrants, and the company senses surrounding enchantments with [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Detect Magic|Detect Magic]]. [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Jingles|Jingles]] entertains and unsettles the companions, while [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Mirage|Mirage]] expresses a wish to experience ordinary living pleasures, including eating pie.

The group experiments with the **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/Other/Ring of Spelling|Ring of Spelling]]**, a custom magic item that lets a caster add a letter to—or replace one letter in—a spell's name to alter what the spell does. [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Weary|Weary]] jokingly changes [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Banishment|Banishment]] into **“Danishment”**, temporarily giving [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Mirage|Mirage]] the name **Dan**. This outcome is part of the in-game magic exchange; it is not evidence that Mirage's underlying identity permanently changes. In the same chaotic exchange, **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Jingles|Jingles]]** casts [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Polymorph|Polymorph]] on [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Noisy Owlbear|Noisy Owlbear]], transforming him into a **baboon**. Owlbear retains his immediate antics but is working with the creature's altered faculties. A projectile hurled by the baboon is deflected by [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Shield|Shield]].

### The first riddle

At the entrance the enormous doors present a word puzzle. The DM gives the group a spoken clue beginning **“A spoken word unseals me”** and refers to a word starting with **T**, a point **halfway between twins**, and a following character. The companions compare possible letters, debate the layout of the writing on the doors, and try to reason their way to a solution. The transcript ends while that discussion is still ongoing; no final answer is audible in the surviving recording.

**Zach's separate handwritten notes, however, report that Owlbear and [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Mirage|Mirage]] solved the riddle and the party entered the mausoleum.** Zach also records that Owlbear then touched an orb and it became something resembling a **map**, with a question mark in the notes. This final interaction is not captured in the surviving audio, and the exact object and outcome should remain open for clarification. The party's first exploration **inside** the mausoleum belongs to the next historical session.

## ✧ Questions & Mysteries

- Wiley tentatively recalled **Jonks** joining the engine hemispheres; the joined object **floated around**. The voice during the sandwich experiment, lasting consequences and whether the halves can separate remain unknown.
- Wiley believes **Machaimia** is the name of **a religion started by Noisy Owlbear**, not necessarily the settlement's formal name. The spelling, connection to Meshites and reason Globby Glore signage was crossed out still require confirmation.
- Who or what bound **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Mirage|Mirage]]'s** soul to a metallic body? What are its abilities and background?
- Who owns the **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/Other/Ring of Spelling|Ring of Spelling]]**, **glass cube of force**, and supposed night-vision goggles? What are the precise limits of the ring's letter-altering effects and the true properties of the other purchases?
- How did **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Jingles|Jingles]]** acquire his restraints and debt, and why did the ancestors react to his release?
- What exact answer opened the doors of the [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Infinite Mausoleum|Infinite Mausoleum]], and did [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Noisy Owlbear|Noisy Owlbear]]'s later orb interaction reveal a map?
