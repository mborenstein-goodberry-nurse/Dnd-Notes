## Log

### ✧ The Lich and Falatir's Bargain

The session opened with the DM recounting how the party had survived the gladiatorial fight against **Susie the Desecrator**, a [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Bestiary/Manticore|manticore]], and a [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Bestiary/Chimera|chimera]]. Afterward, a hydra emerged from another holding area and was killed almost immediately by a lich using *[[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Finger of Death|Finger of Death]]*. This was a **recap of the previous session**, rather than a newly played confrontation. The lich was later identified in the group's historical notes as [[Worlds/Astodan/People/Xerac|Xerac]]. He referred to the late [[Worlds/Astodan/People/Duke Ingrail|Duke Ingrail]] as a supplier of bodies or corpses; his exact arrangement with Ingrail remained unclear.

[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Falatir|Falatir]] noticed a shard hanging around the lich's neck and recognized it as something he needed. The lich demanded an extraordinary price: in exchange for the shard, Falatir's **soul and service would belong to the lich after Falatir's death**. The contract's explanation added that if the lich died, the claim would pass to a successor, though no current successor was identified. Falatir inspected the contract, considered warnings from his companions, and **signed it**. When Falatir brought the shard near a card he carried, the shard was drawn into it and a **new card** appeared. Later remarks indicate that the original and new cards depicted the same woman in different emotional states; what the images meant was not explained to the group.

Xerac then offered the surviving adventurers a way out. He claimed to have been confined to the necropolis for centuries and proposed releasing them on condition that they take charge of several individuals who had recently come into his custody. The newcomers introduced themselves: **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Grayson|Grayson Underwood]]**, a human warlock carrying a staff and deck of cards (the original journal also reports a small imp companion); **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Krieg|Krieg]]**, a confident tiefling; and **[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Cain|Cain]]**, a pale, elf-featured man with a red mechanical eye, a mechanical right arm equipped with a gun, and a prosthetic leg. Cain said he was searching for his **brother**. His brother's identity was not given. After introductions, Xerac departed, and the group emerged from the crumbling [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Hallowed Bastion|Hallowed Bastion]] into the dawn.

### ✧ From the Gilded Lands to the Hidden Towers

The adventurers recovered their belongings and returned to the couple's [[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/Other/Aceso Aid|Aceso Aid]] caravan. They traveled through the outer [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Gilded Lands|Gilded Lands]] rather than returning to its cities, continuing the mission assigned by [[Worlds/Astodan/People/Queen Isabel|Queen Isabel]]. The countryside gave way to cold, distorted woodland near the border of the [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Dwarven Forge|Dwarven Forge]]. At first the [[Worlds/Astodan/Places/Towers of Icarus|Towers of Icarus]] were invisible. A careful inspection revealed brass-and-gold towers obscured by powerful magical enchantment and an unsettling corruption of what seemed once to have been divine or celestial magic.

[[Campaigns/Sands of Desolation/The Party/Weary|Weary]] used *[[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Dispel Magic|Dispel Magic]]* to strip away the concealing veil, making the towers visible to everyone. Because they stood within a corrupted magical wellspring, the spell triggered a **harmful wild-magic surge** that summoned a hostile **[[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Bestiary/Glabrezu|Glabrezu]]**. The party fought the demon; the supplied B06 recording ends mid-encounter and B07 resumes after that fight, so its precise defeat is not independently heard. While traveling farther into the thicket, the party sensed pressure upon their minds. One companion briefly spoke in a distorted voice warning them to leave. Trees shifted to make a path toward three connected towers, a short one, an intermediate one, and a tall central spire. Some adventurers glimpsed a large, bleach-white figure with purple-corrupted veins watching from above before it disappeared.

### ✧ The Invisible Captive

The group entered through the broad doors of the shortest tower, leaving the wagon outside. A hidden poison-dart trap struck several companions on the stairs. Upstairs, they discovered a pedestal apparently standing empty, although magic and thrown objects revealed a **permanent invisibility field** around something tangible. Several attempts to inspect it—including a spectral hand and *[[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Faerie Fire|Faerie Fire]]*—failed to explain what lay within. In a notorious display of curiosity, **Arthur and Cain urinated at the pedestal and invisible figure**. Weary made to join them, but **Diane held him back**. The surviving audio does *not* show Weary urinating on the captive despite that claim in the old written journal.

Cain entered the invisible field, followed by other curious companions, and found a celestial figure the group initially called a "diva" (the recorded word could equally sound like "deva"; no such identity was confirmed). The figure's wings were clipped and it barely moved or spoke. Diane found a Celestial inscription implying that the being was **bound there until someone desired its worth**. A companion trying to leave the tower found himself unable to go down the stairs. When Cain struck or touched the captive with his mechanical hammer-arm, it seized and held him. After an attempt to use *[[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Spells/Lesser Restoration|Lesser Restoration]]*, the invisibility dropped and the figure was revealed as a **[[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Bestiary/Radiant Idol|Radiant Idol]]**, rather than having been identified by its earlier nickname. It immediately attacked Cain; the fight began in the available B07 audio, including a charm effect that prevented one companion from attacking it.

**Consequences and possessions:** Falatir acquired a second card from the shard and accepted a dangerous after-death bond to Xerac. The party escaped captivity and recovered its belongings, while new allies joined the company. Poison darts and a wild-magic [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Bestiary/Glabrezu|Glabrezu]] encounter caused immediate danger; the [[The Wanderer's Guide to the Multiverse/Bestiary/Radiant Idol|Radiant Idol]] battle continued across the recording boundary. The precise class features and mechanics of the idol, demon, and shard have not been inferred from their names.
