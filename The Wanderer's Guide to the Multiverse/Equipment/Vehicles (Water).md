Water vehicles range from humble fishing boats to ocean-crossing ships. They carry trade, armies, travelers, and explorers over rivers, lakes, and open seas, where wind, currents, and weather are as important as the skill of the crew.

## Proficiency

**Vehicles (Water)** is a tool proficiency in the 2014 D&D 5e rules. A proficient character knows how to operate and maintain watercraft, including the practical work of a sailor: rigging, knots, seamanship, and recognizing hazards on the water. The character adds their proficiency bonus to appropriate ability checks to control a water vehicle in difficult circumstances.

This is a **different proficiency** from [[Vehicles (Land)]]. [[Navigator's Tools]] can complement it, but plotting a course and handling a ship are not necessarily the same task.

| Vessel | Typical Use |

Their 2014 reference prices and movement rates appear in [[Transportation]].

## Using Water Vehicles

Proficiency may help when steering through reefs, handling a vessel in heavy weather, assessing damage, docking in a strong current, or coordinating crew maneuvers. Related checks may involve [[Perception]], [[Investigation]], or [[Survival]], depending on the task.

*Xanathar's Guide to Everything* suggests DC 10 to navigate rough waters and DC 15 to judge a vehicle's condition. The DM determines when a check is needed and which ability applies.

The 2014 travel rules treat waterborne vessels differently from parties walking overland: a ship travels at its own speed and may be capable of operating through the night, depending on crew and vessel. See [[Travel Pace]].
