Land vehicles carry travelers and cargo over roads, plains, and rough country. From a farmer's cart to a noble's carriage or a war chariot, their usefulness depends as much on the skill of the driver as on the strength of the animals or machinery pulling them.

## Proficiency

**Vehicles (Land)** is a tool proficiency in the 2014 D&D 5e rules. A proficient character understands how to operate, inspect, maintain, and make routine repairs to land vehicles. When circumstances make controlling one difficult, the character adds their proficiency bonus to an appropriate ability check, as determined by the DM.

This proficiency is **separate** from [[Vehicles (Water)]]. It also doesn't automatically grant proficiency in [[Animal Handling]]: managing a frightened horse and steering a carriage through a narrow pass may call for different checks.

## Common Vehicles

- **Cart:** A practical, usually two-wheeled conveyance for supplies and trade goods.
- **Wagon:** A heavier vehicle well suited to merchants, expeditions, and overland caravans.
- **Carriage:** An enclosed or passenger-focused conveyance used on established roads.
- **Chariot:** A fast, open vehicle that can be used in races, ceremonies, or war.
- **Sled:** A vehicle designed to travel over snow, ice, or other suitable surfaces.

Standard prices and weights appear in [[Transportation]].

## Using Land Vehicles

Vehicle proficiency is useful for navigating broken roads, maneuvering in crowded streets, judging the condition of wheels and axles, or reacting when a vehicle begins to overturn. Knowledge of the vehicle may also aid suitable [[Investigation]] or [[Perception]] checks.

*Xanathar's Guide to Everything* offers examples of vehicle tasks: navigating difficult terrain (DC 10), assessing a vehicle's condition (DC 15), and taking a tight corner at high speed (DC 20). These are suggested difficulties, not mandatory checks for ordinary travel.

For overland distances, consult [[Travel Pace]].
